\vspace{2pt}

\textbf{What this is.} The study asks how fast a news recommender built from past clicks goes stale, which of three
signals (word matching, meaning matching, click history) goes stale first, and how often a rebuild pays off. This note
lays out the decisions taken so far as a tree: each node is a question, the branch taken is shaded, the alternatives
rejected are grey with the reason, and open questions are red. \textbf{Shading:}
\colorbox{dec}{decided}\ \colorbox{prp}{proposed and acted on}\ \colorbox{opn}{open}\ \colorbox{rej}{rejected}.
No decay result exists yet; every number below is a measurement of the data, not of a model.

\section{What is built, what is open}

\textbf{Built and measured} (\texttt{code/}, 30 tests passing): stores for the three datasets (EB-NeRD 113.4\,M clicks;
NPR 17.6\,M, including all 1,402,576 impression clicks; Adressa 35.2\,M), GPU embeddings for all three, the fixed
sample of 20,000 clicks a day for 11--24 May, the model-free curves reproduced from the stores, and five scorers
with a seeded shuffle on one smoke cell. \textbf{Not built:} the leakage and determinism gate (T5), the decay matrix
(T6), the hourly stage (T6h), the analysis (T7), the rebuild-schedule replay (T8), the second dataset run (T9).

\textbf{Open decisions} (red nodes): (1) tell the instructor the dataset changed (D14, low stakes); (2) whether the
headline metric counts unscorable clicks or reports the scorable clicks beside the share scorable (I would report both);
(3) who owns which stage; (4) whether to build the hybrid scorer and the top-$K$ co-click fallback, which were cut from
the first pass. \textbf{Checks that remain:} an unexplained result in the smoke cell (a fresher frozen profile scored
slightly lower for meaning and word matching, finding P41) and the EB-NeRD time zone, which the daily pattern suggests is UTC
but which is not proven.

\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{Rejected, with the reason kept:} Adressa as primary (no impression lists, unfinished download at the time); a
mixed or downsampled corpus (confounds staleness with data volume, or event type); a daily grid alone (the transition
is over within hours on EB-NeRD); factorisation first (it would hide the zero-for-unseen mechanism under test); slate
order as a tie-break (it may carry the site's own ranking).
